# Survey Instrument

Post-play questionnaire, administered immediately after the participant reaches an ending. Participants should not be told which condition (`reactive` or `random`) they played. Recommended delivery: a short online form (e.g. Google Forms/Microsoft Forms), one screen, no more than 2–3 minutes to complete.

Suggested intro text for the form:

> Thanks for playing *Kessler Point*. This short survey asks about your experience with the story you just played. There are no right or wrong answers — we're interested in your honest impression. It should take about 2 minutes.

---

## Section A — Coherence (quantitative)

All items use a 5-point Likert scale: **1 = Strongly disagree, 2 = Disagree, 3 = Neutral, 4 = Agree, 5 = Strongly agree.**

**A1.** The events of the story felt connected to each other, rather than feeling like a series of unrelated scenes.

**A2.** The ending felt like a natural consequence of the choices I made earlier in the story.

*(A1 and A2 are averaged into a single "perceived coherence" score per participant for analysis.)*

## Section B — Perceived influence / illusion of agency

**B1.** My choices during the story meaningfully shaped what happened later. *(1–5 Likert, as above)*

**B2.** If you played again and made different choices at every decision point, how different do you think the story would be? *(1 = Not different at all, 5 = Completely different)*

*(B1/B2 let us check, per condition, whether players over- or under-estimate their actual influence — particularly whether random-condition players still report high perceived influence, i.e. an illusion-of-agency effect, per Day & Zhu, 2017.)*

## Section C — Open-ended (qualitative)

**C1.** Was there a specific moment in the story that felt especially *coherent* (made sense given what came before) or especially *jarring* (didn't fit)? Please describe it briefly.

**C2.** Is there anything else about how the story responded (or didn't respond) to your choices that you'd like to mention?

## Section D — Participant background (for sample description only)

**D1.** Roughly how often do you play narrative-driven or choice-based games (visual novels, interactive fiction, story-heavy RPGs, etc.)?
☐ Never ☐ Rarely ☐ Occasionally ☐ Often ☐ Very often

**D2.** Age range: ☐ Under 16 ☐ 16–17 ☐ 18–24 ☐ 25–34 ☐ 35+
*(Participants under 16 should not be included in this study — see recruitment note below.)*

---

## Recruitment note

Participants should be recruited only from spaces where it is reasonable to expect respondents are 16 or older (university game-development networks, interactive-fiction community spaces). No personally identifying information is collected by this instrument. D2 exists only to confirm the minimum-age criterion and describe the sample, not to identify individuals.

## Scoring reference

| Score | Source items | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence score | mean(A1, A2) | Primary dependent variable; compared reactive vs random |
| Perceived-influence score | mean(B1, B2) | Secondary measure; checked for illusion-of-agency effect in the random condition |
| Qualitative themes | C1, C2 | Used to interpret/contextualise the quantitative result |
